Part one

What changes.

Nothing about the food, the register, or the dine-in flow. One thing changes: where an online order happens and whose page it happens on.

The order page, on the bar's own site

The full menu as Toast sells it today: the burgers with their side choice and egg styles, the wings with all seven sauces and the naked options, the taco proteins including barbacoa, the thirty-line topping lists, the dessert, the cocktails to go and the Faygo. Same prices, same photos, the bar's own brand around all of it. Sold out tonight shows within seconds of the kitchen saying so.

The kitchen board

One PIN screen on any phone or tablet behind the bar. New orders land with a chime that repeats until somebody taps Accept, and that tap tells the guest their food is on. The 86 board takes a dish off the order page in one tap and brings it back in one tap. A busy dial adds fifteen or thirty minutes to the quoted time on a slammed night, and Pause stops ordering entirely, with a timer so it can never be left off overnight by accident. Every tap saves itself.

Cocktails to go, done properly

Michigan made cocktails to go permanent, and the Toast page already sells them. Ours does too, with a guardrail the Toast page does not show: an order with drinks requires the guest to confirm 21 and over at checkout, and the kitchen ticket carries an ID CHECK flag. The carding still happens at the counter, where it always has.

Tickets print exactly where they print today

Two printers, front of house and kitchen, and they are different documents, matching how Toast prints for you now: the kitchen gets the pass ticket, big type and no prices, and the front gets the handoff slip with totals and paid status. A successful kitchen print marks the order accepted by itself, and if a printer ever goes quiet, the system notices within a minute. Nobody watches a tablet unless they want to.

Part four

What it costs.

It costs the bar zero dollars a month, and setup is on the house. Copper is the founding house, and the 50/50 split on the order fee is locked permanently. The 99 cent fee guests pay is the same mechanism every ordering platform already runs, at the lowest number in the industry; half stays in the till, and the other half is what keeps this free.

Card processing runs at the standard published rate, 2.9% + 30 cents, on the bar's own account with next-day settlement. The two printers, front and kitchen to match how tickets print today, are about $310 each, once, at cost, and they can pay themselves off out of the bar's half of the fee before a dollar changes hands.

The demo takes no payment today. Turning real payments on is a fifteen minute signup with the bar's bank details, and it is the one thing on this page we cannot do without you.
