Section one

What it adds.

Start here, because this is the part that is genuinely new. None of these is a tidier version of something the current site has. The current setup cannot run any of them.

The Board

Live results and what is on tonight for the Tigers, Lions, Pistons and Red Wings, laid out like the odds board over a sportsbook counter. It pulls straight from ESPN, so there is no account to keep, nothing to type in and nothing to forget. It refreshes itself, in season and out.

A bar website normally gets looked at once: somebody needs the hours or the address, finds them, and never comes back. A board of Detroit scores is a reason to open it again, and it is the most sports-bar thing a sports bar site could possibly have. No template ships it.

On Tap, live from the bar

All sixteen handles, with the brewery, the strength and a line about each beer, under a green LIVE marker that only shows when the list is actually current. The bar keeps it from a phone: tap a beer, tap 86 it, and it is off the website within a minute. Running low gets a flag. The next keg can be lined up before it goes on.

The rule underneath is the honest one. If the board is ever empty or unreachable, the site does not show a guess; it shows Taps rotate, ask what is pouring, which is what the old site said every day of its life.

Section two

What it fixes.

Six things, in the order they cost the bar something, found by going through the current site page by page in August and checked again on 8 September. Every one links to the page it is on, so none of it has to be taken on trust.

The site never says when the kitchen closes

The hours page lists 11AM to 12AM and stops there. The kitchen closes at ten. Someone reading that page at half nine has no way to know they have thirty minutes, and the Toast ordering page quietly says Closes at 10PM on a screen they never see. Both numbers are right. Nothing on the site puts them together.

Google still thinks the events page is about Copper Bash 2023

The page itself is current. But the description search engines and link previews read has never been updated. It still says Copper Bash 2023 - Join us Saturday, September 23rd for our annual Copper Bash. Text the events link to someone and that is the blurb that comes up, three years later. Somebody edited that page on 30 July 2026, so this is not neglect. It is a part of the page nobody can see from inside the editor.

Two other companies get the last word on the homepage

Scroll to the very bottom and the page hands off to somebody else. First a Facebook panel, in Facebook’s white-and-blue, with a Follow Page button and a Share button and a video of wings. Section three

Why it happens.

This is the part worth understanding, because it explains most of them at once and it is not about effort.

The events page was edited on 30 July 2026, and the visible page is fine. Somebody is in there keeping it going. Yet the blurb Google and Facebook read off that same page still describes Copper Bash 2023.

That is the whole thing in one example. The editor shows you the part of the page you can see, and quietly keeps a second copy for search engines and link previews that you have to go looking for. So the visible half gets updated and the invisible half sits there for three years. The hours are the same shape of problem: someone typed them once, correctly, and there was nowhere to put the fact that the kitchen stops two hours earlier.

You cannot fix that with more discipline. It has to be the site's job. On the rebuild, every fact lives in one place and every page reads it, so there is nowhere for a second copy to hide.

Section four

You own it. All of it.

Written down, not implied. When the build fee is paid in full, these are yours: the design, the source code, the content, the accounts, the code repository, the hosting project, and every login to them. The domain stays registered in your name and transfers to any registrar you name, free, whenever you ask. After launch it is month to month, thirty days’ notice, no penalty, and the site keeps working on the way out the door. The terms are one plain-English page published at glazedweb.com/agreement, the same page every client gets.

Nothing underneath the site is rented. No page-builder license, no form service, no plugin bill arriving in a year. If a feature can be written instead of rented, we write it. Online ordering stays on Toast because that is where your kitchen already is; every Order Online button on the new site goes to the same Toast page the old one does.

Three questions worth asking any web vendor, including us

1. If we stop paying you, what do we keep? 2. Whose name is the domain registered in? 3. Can we leave in thirty days? Our answers are everything, yours, and yes. Ask whoever looks after the current site the same three, and hold us to ours in person.

What is behind the sign-in

A page of the site called the workroom, locked to the club with one passcode, built for whoever runs your events and social media. Nobody has to call us to change what the site says.

  • Events. Name, date and times, a price line, the Toast ticket link, a photo or flyer straight off the phone, a few bullets. Save, and the events page, the homepage card and the Google listing follow within seconds. A draft stays off the site until it is ready.
  • The menu. Every item with a price box, a description box and an on-or-off switch. Clearing a price puts the printed one back. Nothing here can break the site.
