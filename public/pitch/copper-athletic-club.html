Part one

What it adds.

Start here, because this is the part that is genuinely new. Neither of these is a tidier version of something they already have. Their current setup cannot run either one.

The Board

Live results and what is on tonight for the Tigers, Lions, Pistons and Red Wings, laid out like the odds board over a sportsbook counter. It pulls straight from ESPN, so there is no account to keep, nothing to type in and nothing to forget. It refreshes itself every fifteen minutes, in season and out.

This is the part worth caring about. A bar website normally gets looked at once: somebody needs the hours or the address, finds them, and never comes back. A board of Detroit scores is a reason to open it again, and it is the most sports-bar thing a sports bar site could possibly have. No template ships it and their current setup cannot run it.

Open now, kitchen till 10

A live status line that reads the clock in Marshall and says what is actually true: open with the kitchen on, then bar open with the kitchen shut after ten, then closed overnight. It appears on every page and nobody maintains it.

It also fixes the one problem on the current site that turns a real customer around at the door, which is the first thing in the next section.

Part two

What it fixes.

Six things, in the order they cost the bar something, found by going through the site page by page. Every one links to the page it is on, so none of it has to be taken on trust.

The site never says when the kitchen closes

The hours page lists 11AM to 12AM and stops there. The kitchen closes at ten. Someone reading that page at half nine has no way to know they have thirty minutes, and the Toast ordering page quietly says Closes at 10PM on a screen they never see. Both numbers are right. Nothing on the site puts them together.

Their hours page ↗

Google still thinks the events page is about Copper Bash 2023

The page itself is current: the trivia night is on it and nothing else. But the description search engines and link previews read has never been updated. It still says Copper Bash 2023 - Join us Saturday, September 23rd for our annual Copper Bash. Text the events link to someone and that is the blurb that comes up, three years later. Somebody edited that page on 30 July 2026, so this is not neglect. It is a part of the page nobody can see from inside the editor.

The menu has not changed since October 2023

And the contact page since September 2023, going by the dates in their own sitemap. Those are the two pages a customer actually needs, and they are the two that have been hardest to touch.

Their sitemap ↗

Sharing the link posts a photo with no name on it

Paste copperac.com into a text and you get a 2021 photo of the taps. No logo, no headline, no address. It is the first thing a stranger sees of the place and it says nothing.

The actual file ↗

Two other companies get the last word on the homepage

Scroll to the very bottom and the page hands off to somebody else. First a Facebook panel, in Facebook’s white-and-blue, with a Follow Page button and a Share button and a video of wings. Then, under it, a Yelp waitlist in Yelp’s red, reading 0 mins next to a green Live dot and offering to remind you to join later. Neither one looks like the bar. They are the only interactive things on the site and they are the last thing anybody sees.

The Facebook panel is the part worth thinking about, because it says 5,910 followers right on it. That is the whole audience, and the website’s answer to them is a button that sends them back to Facebook. The site is treating itself as the least interesting place to be, and on the current evidence it is right. That is the argument for the Board above: give people something here and they stay here.

Part three

Why it happens.

This is the part worth understanding, because it explains most of them at once and it is not about effort.

Their events page was edited on 30 July 2026, nine days before we looked at it, and the visible page is fine. Somebody is in there keeping it going. Yet the blurb Google and Facebook read off that same page still describes Copper Bash 2023.

That is the whole thing in one example. The editor shows you the part of the page you can see, and quietly keeps a second copy of it for search engines and link previews that you have to go looking for. So the visible half gets updated and the invisible half sits there for three years. The hours are the same shape of problem: someone typed them once, correctly, and there was nowhere to put the fact that the kitchen stops two hours earlier.

You cannot fix that with more discipline. It has to be the site's job.

Part five

What happens next.

1

Have a look at it

The whole thing is standing up at copperac.glazedweb.com/demo. Open it on a phone, at the bar, on a Sunday morning. Check the hours against the clock.

2

Tell us what is wrong

A dish described wrong, a photo that is not theirs any more, a line that does not sound like them. That list is worth more to us than a compliment and it is cheaper to fix now.

3

Then a conversation

Half an hour, screen to screen, and any question about how it is built or who owns it. Short answer on the second one: they do.

No deadline on this page and nothing to sign. If the answer is no, the six things above are still true and still worth handing to whoever looks after the site today. Copper Athletic Club’s logo and photographs belong to them and are used here with permission, to show them their own site.
